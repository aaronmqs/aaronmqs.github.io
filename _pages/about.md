I am a PhD candidate in Aerospace and Mechanical Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, in the [Computational Mechanics and Optimization Laboratory](https://mjzahr.github.io/about-home.html) advised by Dr. Matthew J. Zahr.

My research is in computational fluid dynamics. I work on high-order discontinuous Galerkin methods for high-speed compressible flows with shocks, with a focus on making PDE-based artificial-viscosity shock capturing robust and free of manual tuning through resolution-aware scaling, adjoint-based error estimation, and optimization. My current work applies scientific machine learning to automate the nonlinear convergence of implicit CFD solvers. I build and run these solvers in Julia with MPI on HPC clusters. I also contributed the computational part of a study of hypersonic inlet/isolator unstart, published in the *Journal of Propulsion and Power*.

Before Notre Dame, I earned an MS and a BS in Electrical Engineering at the Federal University of Ceará, Brazil. My MS dissertation studied the stability of PI-controlled systems with time-varying delays.

I am open to internship and full-time roles in computational fluid dynamics and scientific computing.
